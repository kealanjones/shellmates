# Art Bible — Shellmates

## Art Direction (Early)
- High-quality cosy pixel art with clear forms and emotional readability.
- Soft, colourful coastal palette with magical accents.
- “Cute danger” over dark horror.

## Phone Readability Rules
- Prioritise readable silhouettes at mobile scale.
- Keep background detail from obscuring interactables.
- UI overlays must not bury key scene actions.

## Character and Lobster Standards
- Characters: simple, charming proportions with readable pose language.
- Lobsters: highly expressive eyes/claws/posture; clear personality through animation.
- Lobsters must feel supportive, playful, and emotionally legible.

## UI Style Direction
- Soft framing, clear hierarchy, minimal clutter.
- Pixel motifs can be decorative but never reduce usability.

## Animation Principles
- Small expressive loops over heavy animation complexity.
- Prioritise readability, emotional cues, and battery-conscious motion.

## Placeholder Art Rules
- Codex may create placeholder specs, naming plans, and asset organisation guidance.
- Placeholder art is temporary and must not be treated as final quality.
- Final high-quality pixel art requires explicit review or dedicated art production process.

## Naming and Folder Expectations
- Use consistent prefixes by domain (e.g., `char_`, `lob_`, `cove_`, `ui_`).
- Keep source files and exported files clearly separated once production begins.
- Maintain revision notes for changed assets.

## Decisions Required Before Production Art
- Exact sprite sizes.
- Final palette values.
- Animation frame budgets by asset class.
- These are open decisions and must be logged before lock-in.

## Future Art Brief Requirements
Each brief must include:
- Gameplay purpose
- Emotional target
- Readability constraints
- Animation needs
- Integration notes
