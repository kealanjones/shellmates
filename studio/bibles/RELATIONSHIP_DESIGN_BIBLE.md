# Relationship Design Bible — Shellmates

## Positioning
Shellmates is couples-first. It aims to support connection through play, not therapy.

## Core Relationship Design Principles
1. Playful connection over emotional labour.
2. Shared progress without blame.
3. Opt-in emotional depth.
4. Flexible pacing for uneven schedules.
5. Safety and dignity for both partners.

## Relationship Mechanics as Game Objects
- Shell notes and letters as expressive artefacts.
- Mood rituals as light check-ins, never obligations.
- Lobster interactions as affectionate asynchronous signals.
- Tidegates as shared adventure milestones.

## Emotional Intensity Levels
- Light: silly and warm.
- Medium: reflective but gentle.
- Brave: deeper or vulnerable prompts.

## Consent and Opt-In Rules
- Brave Shells must be opt-in or clearly signposted.
- Players must be able to swap prompt depth.
- Provide “make it lighter” and “save for later” options for deeper content.

## Prompt Categories
- Silly Shells
- Warm Shells
- Memory Shells
- Future Shells
- Support Shells
- Brave Shells
- Date Shells
- Letter Shells

## Deeper Prompt Handling
- Never drop conflict/repair prompts unexpectedly during light play.
- Let players preview tone before opening deeper prompts.
- Allow skip/swap without penalty.

## Uneven Partner Activity
- Do not assign blame to absent or less active partner.
- Provide solo alternatives that still feel meaningful.
- Shared gate copy should encourage patience and warmth.

## Long-Distance Considerations
- Time-zone tolerant progression windows.
- Lightweight asynchronous actions with clear delayed payoff.
- Support signalling care without requiring synchronous presence.

## One-Player-Only Task Rules
- One-player tasks may earn preparation resources and optional support actions.
- One-player tasks must not complete major shared story outcomes alone.

## Asynchronous Shared Progression Rules
- Main chapter progression requires contribution from both partners.
- Optional support actions should feel kind, not mandatory.
- System language should celebrate contribution, not monitor inactivity.

## Good Language Examples
- “The tide is gathering.”
- “Your lobster is keeping watch.”
- “The lantern is half-lit; one more spark will open the way.”

## Bad Language Examples
- “Your partner is waiting for you.”
- “You missed your streak.”
- “Your relationship health dropped.”

## Forbidden Patterns
- Relationship scoring systems.
- Compatibility percentages.
- Punitive streak loops.
- Diagnostic/therapeutic claims.
- Forced deep prompts.
